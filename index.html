    How it is made

    Every piece of furniture is a 3D model. The drawers move on joints.

    A flat picture cannot be opened. Stash needs real 3D geometry, split into parts, with a joint on each moving part. That is the job Lux3D does here.

      What is real, and what is not yet.

      Is this a real room?

      Not yet. The demo room is a placeholder laid out by hand. The furniture in it is real Lux3D output, generated from text, with sizes from Lux3D's size prediction. The first room scanned from a phone video replaces it.

      Do the drawers really move?

      They move on joints with a type, an axis and limits. In the placeholder room the moving parts are boxes and the limits are estimates. Part split and articulation replace both with Lux3D output.

      Is the robot an AI?

      It is a simulated robot with a stated search rule: open the spot with the best chance per second of effort, then rule it out. Its odds start from container size and type and shift with logged hides. It does not learn beyond that.

      Does playing cost anything?

      No. The game replays saved results. Only the optional live generation calls the API, with a daily cap.

      What happens to my hides?

      They stay in your browser, where they nudge the robot's odds for you. Nothing is sent to a server.
